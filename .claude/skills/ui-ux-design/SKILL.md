# UI/UX design

## Direction

Luxury European interiors: quiet, warm and precise. Walnut accents on warm beige and ivory, charcoal
text, generous white space, large images, thin rules instead of boxes, few but deliberate accents.

## Layout patterns

- Asymmetric editorial grids (12 columns) rather than rows of identical cards.
- One idea per section, with a clear heading, a short paragraph and one action.
- Large type for headings; body text 16–18 px, line length 60–75 characters.
- Product pages: hero → what we make → materials and finishes → process → projects → enquiry.

## Imagery

- Approved company photography only (see `gik-content-rules`). Without photos, use typographic
  layouts or illustrated swatches clearly marked as illustrations — never stock interiors presented
  as projects.
- Consistent aspect ratios per context (16:9 hero, 4:5 portrait cards, 1:1 swatches).

## Conversion paths

Every page offers the next step without pressure: request a quotation, book a consultation, visit the
showroom, or WhatsApp. The quotation wizard shows progress, keeps entered data on errors, and never
asks for information it doesn't use.

## Forms

Labels above fields, examples as hints, inline errors after blur, one primary button, confirmation
pages with the reference number and what happens next.

## Arabic

Arabic layouts mirror direction but keep images and numbers sensible; Arabic headings are often
shorter, so check balance; use Arabic-Indic numerals only if the owner prefers them.
